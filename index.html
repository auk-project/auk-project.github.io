Auk: A Unified Foundational Model for Speech Generation and Editing

One model, one natural-language interface, and five task families: speech generation, content editing, enhancement and separation, paralinguistic editing, and acoustic editing. Auk ships with Auk-Flash, a 4-step distilled variant that keeps the same capabilities at a 4.5× speedup.

    A unified interface for every audible transformation

    Auk consumes a natural-language instruction and an optional reference audio, and emits a 24-kHz waveform. The same backbone covers all five task families below.

      Architecture

      A multimodal language model provides semantic conditioning, a 50 Hz VAE supplies reference acoustic latents, and a FLUX-style hybrid transformer fuses them through dual-stream MMDiT blocks and single-stream DiT blocks.

      MLLM semantic condition

      Qwen2.5-Omni encodes the instruction jointly with the optional reference audio. A learnable weighted sum of the layer-wise hidden states is layer-normalized to form the semantic condition.

      VAE acoustic latents

      A flow-augmented 24 kHz VAE encodes audio to 50 Hz, 64-dim latents. Its encoder provides the reference branch; the decoder reconstructs the final 24 kHz waveform.

      Hybrid transformer

      30 layers, 1.5B parameters. The first 10 are dual-stream MMDiT blocks with RoPE and stream-specific residuals. The remaining 20 are single-stream DiT blocks over the concatenated sequence.

      Pre-training scale

      3.03B instruction–audio instances spanning 1.95M hours. 600k updates on 256 H20 GPUs, 32-sample micro-batches, 50 Hz flow matching biased toward the low-noise regime.

      Auk-Flash: 4 steps, 4.5× faster

      Auk-Flash is distilled from Auk in two stages. A trajectory-level consistency initialization hands a few-step student over to a task-routing decoupled DMD objective, which supervises separation samples with a clean-prediction regression and keeps them out of the DMD loop. The result preserves the base model's editing breadth while removing the inference cost.
